# AURA — health monitoring when help is far away

NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026 · *Create Health Monitoring Software for Astronauts on Space Missions*

Live: https://auraspace.space · 3-minute guided tour with simulated data: https://auraspace.space/?demo=1

AURA helps people assess their own health when a doctor is minutes or months away: astronauts on a long mission, crews at a polar station, and older adults at home. It offers a 3D body map, status-coded vital signs (manual entry, a Samsung Health import or a live Arduino cabin-sensor kit), alerts that lead straight to the next action, trend-based early warning, an offline rule-based symptom consultation with sourced first-aid guidance, a personalised exercise programme, live NASA space-weather data, a voice assistant and optional webcam hand control. Advice takes the communication delay to Earth into account. Urgent alerts and every other health decision come from deterministic rules, never from a language model.

## Run locally

```bash
npm install
npm run dev      # demo mode: data stays in the browser, no server needed
npm test         # unit tests
```

## Use of Super Intelligence (SI, formerly called AI)

**In the product.** No language model or other SI makes any health decision. The health score, alerts, delay-aware triage, early warning, the NEWS2 card, the symptom consultation, medicine checks, the coach and what the voice assistant says are deterministic rules: the same input always gives the same output. Machine-learning components that do exist are used for input and output only:
- MediaPipe Hand Landmarker (Google) runs on the device for the optional hand-gesture control. Camera frames are processed locally and never sent or stored.
- The browser's speech recognition (Web Speech API). In Chrome and Edge it sends audio to the browser vendor's cloud service and needs internet.
- The browser's or operating system's text-to-speech reads replies and the guided tour aloud. Some browsers use neural voices provided by their vendor.

**In making the project.** Most of the code, the documentation and the sourced knowledge base in this repository were generated by SI coding assistants under our direction: Anthropic Claude via Claude Code, with several sub-agents working in parallel. Every commit carries a `Co-Authored-By: Claude …` line as metadata. OpenAI Codex was used once as a reviewer; its three comments were checked and implemented with Claude (commit `8e12d2d`). At the start of the hackathon we also used SI chat assistants for a challenge summary, idea options, a roadmap and a task split. Our team chose the direction and the features, supplied the hardware, set up the hosting, tested the app on our own devices, reported the problems that were then fixed, and decided what was published.

## Offline: what works and what does not

The rule engines run in the browser without a connection, and the app caches itself after the first visit. These need a network: live space weather (it falls back to the last saved copy, shown with its age), speech recognition in Chrome and Edge, e-mail codes and syncing with the server. Fonts fall back to system fonts when offline.

## Data and sources

- **Live:** NASA CCMC DONKI (solar flares, solar energetic particle events, coronal mass ejections, geomagnetic storms, notifications) and NOAA SWPC (space-weather scales, GOES proton flux), fetched and cached by our server (`public/api/routes/spaceweather.php`). DONKI describes its content as experimental research information; NOAA SWPC is the official U.S. forecast source. AURA does not compute a radiation dose from these events.
- **Evidence base:** NASA Human Research Program pages, NASA OCHMO technical briefs, NASA-STD-3001, NASA NTRS reports, CSA and ESA pages, and public-health sources (NHS, CDC, WHO, NICE, the Royal College of Physicians' NEWS2). The consultation, medicine, coach, early-warning and environment guidance links its source, with the quoted sentence, inside the app. Rules that are our own modelling are labelled "AURA rule".
- **Simulated people:** the crew, the polar member and the home-care member in the demo are simulated. Values tagged Simulated, Sample or Synthetic are not real measurements. The radiation dose figure is a simple mission-profile model, not a measurement.

## Notes

- AURA supports self-assessment and decision making. It does not diagnose, has not been clinically validated and is not a medical device.
- Not affiliated with or endorsed by NASA. No NASA logo or insignia is used.
- The cabin sensor kit measures temperature, humidity and a raw flammable-gas/smoke value. It is not a carbon-monoxide or CO₂ sensor and not a certified alarm.
